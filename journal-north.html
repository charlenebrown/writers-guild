THE WRITING LIFE

Black stories belong in northern landscapes, too.

Imani Okafor · Oct 02, 2026 · 4 min read

Let the place be lived in.

The first question people ask about my poems is often about the weather. I want to tell them about friendship. About a gathering after work. About a neighbor who noticed when I had gone quiet.

Resist the single story.

A landscape can shape the work without becoming the only thing the work is allowed to discuss. Black writing in Alaska can be funny, intimate, restless, domestic, and concerned with the same impossible questions found everywhere else.

Connection takes intention.

Distance asks a writing community to work differently. Online circles, shared reading, and regular check-ins can turn an isolated practice into a sustained conversation.

Begin where your life is.

You do not need to leave your place to make it worthy of a poem. Begin with what you notice. Then ask what makes the detail matter to the person who lives beside it.
