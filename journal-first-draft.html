CRAFT NOTES

The first draft is allowed to have a pulse before a plan.

Theo Morgan · Sep 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Give the page something alive.

A first draft does not need to know how the whole story ends. It needs a voice, a pressure, a person who wants something. Start there before you ask the page to become impressive.

Protect a small appointment.

A repeatable fifteen minutes can do more for the work than a perfect afternoon that never arrives. Put the appointment somewhere your actual life can support it.

Choose one useful question.

When you return to a draft, ask one question at a time. What changed? What is the character avoiding? Where does the voice become more specific? Revision works better when attention has a direction.

Share at the right stage.

Not every page needs an audience immediately. A workshop should help you hear the work more clearly, not convince you to surrender the part that matters to you.
